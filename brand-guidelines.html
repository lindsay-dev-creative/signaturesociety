Signature
Society

Brand guidelines covering the typographic system and the color system. Every specimen on this page is rendered in the live token it documents, so what you see is what ships. Resize the window to watch the responsive scale change.

Digital

Everything from Typefaces through Form typography specifies type for screen: the responsive scale, the pixel sizes and the interface styles. Print will follow as its own part. Where the two differ, the part a spec sits in is what governs it.

Typefaces

Digital

Kepler Std carries the brand voice and is used at four optical sizes. Choosing the optical size by point size is not optional: it is what keeps the letterforms correct at scale. Inter is the secondary face, used for interface copy, forms and the secondary button.

Secondary type scale

Digital

Inter at weight 400, base 15px. There are no steps above the base: anything larger belongs to Kepler Std. This scale does not change across breakpoints. Use it for interface copy that should recede, and anywhere Kepler would feel too editorial.

Button type

Digital

Two button type styles. The primary carries Kepler Std, the secondary carries Inter, and both sit at 14px so a side-by-side pair aligns on the same optical line. Neither changes across breakpoints.

Form typography

Digital

Forms are entirely Inter. Kepler Std is never used inside a form control: checkout is a task, not a place for the brand voice. The form styles are built from the secondary scale and do not change across breakpoints.

Color system

Six families running 950 at the darkest to 50 at the lightest. Five carry eleven steps; Neutral carries twelve, with an extra 25 above the 50. The brand swatch is marked with an inset rule and a bullet, and it is not the same step in every family: 900 for Primary, Red, Green and Orange, 500 for Tan, 100 for Neutral. Each family heading names its own. Click any swatch to copy its hex.

Action required · primary/950 duplicates primary/900

primary/950 and primary/900 are both #05060F. Every other family has a distinct 950, so the darkest Primary step reads as unset rather than deliberate. Checked against both the bound variables and the rendered swatches on the Brand Color Scale frame, so it is in the file rather than a reporting artefact. Primary has ten usable steps until it is given its own 950.

The family is named red in Figma and is called Red here to match, though the role it plays in the brand is the secondary one.
